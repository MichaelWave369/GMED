# STARTRACE-5 Rung 2 — Ancient candidate sky lab

**Status:** Approximate, reproducible, three-dimensional candidate-trajectory visualization and topocentric alt/az comparison. **Not** a validated reproduction of the actual ancient sky, not a historical comet orbit determination, and not a visibility or probability claim.

## Primary inputs

- Mark Matney (2025), *The star that stopped: The Star of Bethlehem & the comet of 5 BCE*, *Journal of the British Astronomical Association* 135(6), pp. 387–406. [Open PDF](https://britastro.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/387-407_Matney_JBAA_2025-1.pdf): page 395 central case, page 397 Tables 1–2 for ten alternative orbits. All comet orbital elements are **J2000 ecliptic**, q in AU, orbital angles in degrees, perihelion as Julian date, parabolic e=1.
- NASA/JPL Standish & Williams, [Approximate Positions of the Planets](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html), Table 2a: Earth/Moon barycenter long-range elements and their rates. Only the interval 3000 BC–3000 AD is used.
- Bethlehem observer (approx.): latitude 31.7043 N, longitude 35.2077 E. Historic observer location is not uniquely recoverable from the account.

## Implementation and chronology

The 3D orbit view uses actual heliocentric J2000 ecliptic XYZ coordinates generated by:

1. NASA's long-range **approximate Earth/Moon barycenter** formulas, converted from JPL elements by solving Kepler's equation.
2. Each published hypothetical comet orbit propagated as a **Sun-only parabola** with Barker's equation. q, omega, ascending node, i, perihelion JD are read from the published tables.
3. A 3D perspective camera with pointer-drag yaw/pitch and reference ecliptic grid. This is a 3D coordinate visualization on a 2D canvas, **not WebGL**.
4. The observed direction is comet minus Earth vector, transformed from J2000 ecliptic to J2000 equatorial then via IAU 1976 approximate precession to the mean equator of date. A WGS84-sized topocentric site offset is subtracted from an **approximate barycenter-derived Earth center**.
5. Sun direction is calculated from negative Earth vector in the same approximate frame. Local mean sidereal time uses a conventional GMST polynomial from Julian UT. Altitude, azimuth and relative distance then follow from local east, north, up dot products.

### Vital UT/TT boundary

The paper's Jun 8 **local noon UT** reference is JD 1719755.898. It is **not** an ephemeris TT timestamp. For a day/hours selected from it, first compute **JD_UT**. Then compute ephemeris **JD_TT = JD_UT + ΔT/86400**, with default ΔT=10,572 seconds. Do **not** subtract ΔT from this UT noon reference; doing so shifts the calculated Sun away from its meridian by hours. A deterministic regression test checks the Sun is near the southern meridian at local noon.

## Interactive controls

- Select any of the 11 published alternatives for June 3–13, 5 BCE. Each candidate is simulated on *its own proposed closest-approach date*, not a common date.
- Adjust local solar hour from 04:00 to 18:00, play/pause a daylight sequence.
- Change the **assumed** ΔT ±600 seconds. This is an illustrative sensitivity slider, **not a quantified uncertainty interval**.
- Toggle the ten other published candidates in both visualizations. The collection is **not a Monte Carlo sample** and does not define probabilities or a confidence band.
- Export transparent JSON: selected original published candidate elements, modeled outputs, source links, limitations, and an explicitly unverified verdict. The R2 receipt has a **new schema** and is *not yet accepted by the R1-only PhiTriBrain WITNESS bridge*.

## Reality checks / why this is not a verified Figure 6 reproduction

The numerical trajectory may resemble the paper's published patterns in the broad sense, but no exact plot fidelity is certified. The model uses a **JPL Earth/Moon barycenter rather than Earth center**, a significant offset during lunar-distance comet encounters. It omits lunar ephemerides, precise Earth barycentric/heliocentric transformations, gravitational focusing by Earth during close approach, nutation, annual and diurnal aberration, non-gravitational comet forces, accurate observer chronology, atmospheric refraction and extinction, photometric daylight contrast, and high-precision integrated ephemerides. The simplified ancient precession/GMST model and approximate ΔT require independent tests against Horizons/DE data.

The scene contains no calibrated ancient star catalog and should not be described as showing the exact stars one would have seen in Bethlehem. The polar plot shows above-horizon geometry, **not naked-eye visibility**, particularly when the Sun is up.
